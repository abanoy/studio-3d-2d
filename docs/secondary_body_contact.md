# Generated hair contact with arms

When a generated HAS loose-parts rig has no imported colliders, Hair & clothes can fit temporary upper- and lower-arm capsules from the model's weighted geometry. Their endpoints follow the actual elbow and wrist. Imported collider declarations keep their existing behavior.

The generated contact path checks whole joint links, then the actual cached, weighted hair surface after live pose smoothing. It corrects existing joint rotations, without moving attachments, stretching joints, changing vertices or weights, or rewriting the saved rig. Parent chains are corrected before chains attached beneath them. The live smoother remembers the constrained pose actually displayed, avoiding repeated corrections from an invisible previous pose. Turning Hair & clothes off releases the cache and restores the authored pose through the existing live-simulation lifecycle.

The surface cache reads geometry once. Each tick transforms its saved influences, skips capsules outside a bounding box, and updates only influences affected by a corrective rotation. It has a limit of 200,000 inspected vertices, 4,096 affected vertices, and 24 corrective rotations per pose. Remaining constraints are counted; these limits do not guarantee a solution for arbitrary clothing or rigs. If the optional cache cannot be built, the ordinary spring/segment simulation remains available, with `contact_info.skin_surface_available=false` and the explicit `skin_reason`.

This path requires positive uniform joint scale and matching mesh/skeleton frames. It uses arm capsules, not triangle-level body collision or swept collision detection. Fixed attachments inside a collider may remain unresolved. Changing blend amounts on a cached helper-influenced mesh, its mesh resource, or its skin binding stops contact with a restart hint. Animated morph geometry is not supported by this first contact increment. The fitted arm radii are a static-geometry approximation; body morphs on separate meshes are outside this qualification.

The protected Miku scene's focused CPU checks cover 480 ticks and all 989 independently calibrated affected hair vertices, with zero penetrating vertex/capsule pairs at the unchanged 0.00005 measurement tolerance. The two-run ordinary flow check also covers exact fixed-step replay, joint positions, source settings, Undo, and Off restoration. The optimized contact code measured 2.98 ms per call on average in that CPU run, versus 73.15 ms in the first direct implementation; this is a component timing, not a real-time frame-rate guarantee. A synthetic 4,097-affected-vertex test verifies the optional-cache fallback preserves ordinary simulation and leaves no resource callbacks attached.

The ordinary graphical On/Off check also measured zero penetrating skin/capsule pairs, balanced readbacks, and unchanged source/settings/Undo. Captures retain the whole model and compare three views of one paused live pose. The tail still bends strongly forward/up under the saved fan, so improved contact does not imply polished hair dynamics. Earlier evidence where links cleared but 1,059 skinned vertex/capsule pairs penetrated remains a failed surface-contact result.
